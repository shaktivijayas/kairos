# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Micro-merchants, freelancers, and gig workers in India — people running a business without an accounting team or ERP system. They forward banking SMS alerts and photograph invoices/cash slips in the flow of their day, on modest Android devices, often without deep fluency in tax jargon or English. The job to be done is ambient: capture a transaction in seconds, then find out later whether it created a compliance problem, without ever having to log into a GSTN portal or set up an integration.

## Product Purpose

KAIROS is an ambient, ERP-free compliance shield. It reads forwarded SMS and photographed invoices to flag Section 40A(3) cash-limit breaches, vendor ITC risk, and unclaimed deductions (80C/80D/24b), showing the reasoning behind every flag. It also answers direct "is this okay?" questions before the user acts. Success is a user who catches a compliance risk or a missed deduction they would otherwise have found out about too late — and who trusts the flag enough to bring it to their CA.

## Positioning

The only compliance tool that needs nothing set up — no ERP integration, no GSTN portal login, just forwarded SMS and a camera — and shows its reasoning for every flag instead of handing down an unexplained verdict.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, trustworthy. KAIROS behaves like a quiet financial safety net, not an alarm system: findings are stated plainly and specifically, severity is legible at a glance, and nothing is designed to spike anxiety over a routine flag. Precision is the trust mechanism — vague warnings are worse than no warning.

## Anti-references

Not a generic fintech SaaS dashboard — no navy-and-chart-heavy enterprise look built for a finance team; KAIROS serves one solo user, not an org. Not a government or tax-portal UI either — no dense forms, bureaucratic language, or GSTN/income-tax-portal friction; avoiding exactly that experience is the product's reason to exist.

## Design Principles

Show the reasoning, never just the verdict — every flag carries the "why," because explainability is the trust mechanism, not a nice-to-have. Advisory, not authoritative — outputs are always framed as worth reviewing with a CA, never as confident tax advice; language and visual weight should never overclaim certainty. Zero setup friction is a design value, not just a technical one — no screen should ask for something the ambient SMS/camera flow doesn't already have. Calm under risk — urgent findings (cash-limit breaches) need to read as clearly more severe than routine ones (deduction opportunities) without the whole interface defaulting to alarm. The primary in-app workflow (scan, ask, dashboard) is the product-register default; a marketing/landing surface within the same app may deliberately switch to brand register for that page only.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor. Given the audience: plain language over tax/finance jargon wherever a plain-language equivalent exists, layouts and asset weights that hold up on modest Android devices and slower mobile networks, and no assumption of strong English fluency — labels and findings should stay short and concrete rather than idiomatic.
